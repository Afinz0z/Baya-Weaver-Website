Before you go

Everything here is within about two hours’ drive of Baya Weaver Resorts in Old Vythiri. Drive times are calculated from the resort’s location using OpenStreetMap road data and assume no traffic, so allow more time on the ghat road, at weekends and in the monsoon.

These are independent places, not part of the resort. Opening days, permits and access change with the seasons and the weather, and some areas were affected by the July 2024 landslides. Check with the operator, the forest department or DTPC Wayanad before you set off.

Views and lakes

Pookode Lake

4 min3 km

A natural freshwater lake ringed by evergreen forest, in the Vythiri area. Pedal boats are usually available, and a path runs around the water.

Open in Google Maps

Lakkidi View Point

5 min4 km

The viewpoint at the top of the ghat road that climbs into Wayanad from Kozhikode, looking down over the hairpin bends and the valleys below. Best early in the morning, before the mist settles or after it lifts.

Open in Google Maps

Chain Tree

5 min4 km

A tree bound with an iron chain beside the highway at Lakkidi. Local legend links it to Karinthandan, a tribal guide said to have shown the British the route up the ghat.

Open in Google Maps

Neelimala View Point

50 min46 km

A hilltop viewpoint near Vaduvanchal with views across the valley towards Meenmutty Falls. The last stretch is a steep track, usually done by local jeep.

Open in Google Maps

Phantom Rock

40 min35 km

A rock formation near Ambalavayal shaped like a skull, with a short climb and wide views over the surrounding plantations.

Open in Google Maps

Waterfalls

Soochipara (Sentinel Rock) Falls

50 min29 km

A three-tiered waterfall near Meppadi, reached by a walk down through tea and forest. The pool at the bottom is popular in the drier months.

This area near Chooralmala was affected by the July 2024 landslides. The falls have reopened, but check current access before you go.

Open in Google Maps

Meenmutty Falls

1 h47 km

A tall, three-tiered waterfall near Vaduvanchal, reached along a jungle trail.

Access has been closed for long periods because of landslide risk. Confirm it is open with the District Tourism Promotion Council (DTPC) before travelling.

Open in Google Maps

Kanthanpara Falls

45 min25 km

A smaller, quieter waterfall near Meppadi, with a short walk from the road. A good choice when the bigger falls are busy.

Open in Google Maps

Thusharagiri Falls

30 min20 km

A series of cascades in forest on the Kozhikode side of the ghat, at their fullest in the months after the monsoon.

Open in Google Maps

Treks and peaks

Chembra Peak

1 h 5 min29 km

Wayanad’s highest peak, known for the heart-shaped lake on the way up. Treks start from the forest office near Meppadi with a forest department guide.

Permits are issued on the day by the forest department, with a daily limit, so start early. Access to the summit has at times been restricted; the lake trek is the usual route.

Open in Google Maps

Banasura Hill

1 h 30 min64 km

A longer forest trek above the Banasura Sagar reservoir, with views over the islands in the lake. Usually arranged with a guide through the forest department or DTPC.

Open in Google Maps

Wildlife

Muthanga (Wayanad Wildlife Sanctuary)

45 min50 km

Forest department jeep safaris run in a morning and an afternoon slot, with a guide. Elephants, deer and gaur are common sightings; tigers are seen occasionally.

Tickets are sold at the forest office on the day and the number of jeeps is limited, so arrive before the slot opens.

Open in Google Maps

Tholpetty (Wayanad Wildlife Sanctuary)

1 h 15 min62 km

The northern range of the sanctuary, near the Karnataka border, with its own jeep safaris. Pairs well with a visit to Thirunelly Temple.

Safaris are limited and run by the forest department; check timings locally.

Open in Google Maps

Heritage and culture

Edakkal Caves

45 min37 km

Rock shelters high on Ambukuthi Mala with carvings thought to date back thousands of years. The final climb is steep and takes most visitors 45 minutes to an hour.

Closed on Mondays, in heavy rain, and once the daily visitor limit is reached. Go early.

Open in Google Maps

Jain Temple, Sulthan Bathery

35 min36 km

A granite temple thought to date from the 13th century. The town’s name is linked to Tipu Sultan, who is said to have used the temple as an ammunition store.

Open in Google Maps

Wayanad Heritage Museum

35 min34 km

A small museum at Ambalavayal with archaeological finds and objects from the district’s tribal communities. Easy to combine with Edakkal Caves and Phantom Rock.

Open in Google Maps

Thirunelly Temple

1 h 5 min60 km

An ancient Maha Vishnu temple in the Brahmagiri hills, visited by pilgrims for ancestral rites at the Papanasini stream. Dress modestly and follow temple customs.

Open in Google Maps

Pazhassi Tomb, Mananthavady

45 min43 km

The memorial of Pazhassi Raja (Kerala Varma), who led resistance to British rule in the region, with a small museum.

Open in Google Maps

En Ooru Tribal Heritage Village

4 min3 km

A tribal heritage village project near Pookode where visitors can learn about the crafts, food and way of life of Wayanad’s tribal communities.

25 min25 km

Thrikkaipetta is home to bamboo workshops where you can see local craftspeople at work and buy handmade pieces.

Rivers, dams and adventure

Banasura Sagar Dam

1 h 30 min64 km

India’s largest earth dam, holding back a reservoir scattered with small islands. Speed boats and pedal boats are usually available.

Kuruva Island

50 min43 km

A cluster of forested river islands on the Kabani, explored on foot after a bamboo raft or boat crossing run by the forest department and DTPC.

Closed through the monsoon and reopened when the river allows; visitor numbers are limited.

The coast and Kozhikode

Thamarassery Ghat

40 min27 km

The road between Wayanad and Kozhikode winds through nine hairpin bends with views over the forest. Worth driving in daylight.

Traffic can back up at weekends and after heavy rain.

The city seafront, busy in the evenings with families, sea views and street food stalls.

Food and drink

Wayanad sits in the Malabar region of northern Kerala, and its food draws on Malabar’s Arab-influenced cooking, Kerala staples, and the coffee, spices and forest produce grown in the hills. Kalpetta and Sulthan Bathery have plenty of local eateries; for the full range of Malabar cooking, Kozhikode is the place.

Kozhikode biryani
Malabar-style dum biryani, made with small-grained rice and cooked sealed so the spices and meat steam together.
Pathiri and Malabar breads
Thin rice-flour flatbreads, eaten with chicken, mutton or fish curries across the Malabar coast.
Kappa and curry
Tapioca, boiled or mashed, served with spicy fish or beef curry. A Kerala staple in small eateries.
Puttu, appam and stew
Breakfast favourites: steamed rice cakes with black chickpea curry, or lacy rice pancakes with a mild coconut stew.
Kerala meals
A full vegetarian lunch of rice with several curries and sides, often served on a banana leaf.
Kozhikode halwa and banana chips
Glossy, chewy halwa in many flavours and chips fried in coconut oil, the classic things to take home.
Sulaimani
Black tea with lemon and spices, the usual after-meal drink in Malabar.
Wayanad coffee, tea, pepper and honey
Wayanad is one of India’s main coffee-growing districts. Local robusta, tea, black pepper, cardamom and forest honey make good gifts.
Bamboo rice
Rice from flowering bamboo, gathered by tribal communities and cooked as porridge or payasam. It is seasonal and not always available.

Sample days out

Ideas for combining nearby places into half days and full days from the resort.

Half a day close to the resort

  1. Pookode Lake
  2. Lakkidi View Point and the Chain Tree
  3. En Ooru Tribal Heritage Village

All within about 5 minutes of the resort. A slow morning or a late afternoon.

A day of caves, rocks and wildlife

  1. Edakkal Caves, early (not on a Monday)
  2. Phantom Rock
  3. Wayanad Heritage Museum
  4. Jain Temple, Sulthan Bathery
  5. Afternoon jeep safari at Muthanga

The south-east of the district, about 40 to 45 minutes away.

A day in north Wayanad

  1. Kuruva Island (outside the monsoon)
  2. Pazhassi Tomb, Mananthavady
  3. Thirunelly Temple
  4. Afternoon safari at Tholpetty

About an hour to an hour and a quarter each way.

Lakes, dams and a waterfall

  1. Karlad Lake
  2. Banasura Sagar Dam
  3. Kanthanpara Falls on the way back

An easy loop with boating and short walks.

A day on the coast

  1. Down the Thamarassery Ghat in daylight
  2. Kozhikode Beach
  3. Lunch in Kozhikode and SM Street
  4. Kappad Beach

About 65 to 75 minutes each way without traffic. Leave early and return before dark.

When to go

  • Monsoon (roughly June to September): heavy rain, green hills and full waterfalls. Some treks, islands and waterfalls close for safety, and roads can be slow.
  • After the rains to early spring (roughly October to February): cool, clearer days and the most places open. The busiest time for visitors.
  • March to May: warmer and drier. Waterfalls are lower, and early starts are best for treks and safaris.

Getting around

  • A car or taxi is the easiest way to reach most places; local jeeps handle the steeper tracks.
  • KSRTC buses connect Kalpetta, Sulthan Bathery, Mananthavady and Kozhikode.
  • The Thamarassery Ghat is the main road to Kozhikode and Calicut International Airport. It is slower than the distance suggests.
  • On the road to Mysore, the stretch of NH766 through Bandipur is closed to most traffic overnight, so plan to cross in daylight.

Travelling responsibly

  • Stay on marked trails and follow forest department guides and rules.
  • Carry your rubbish out. Many forest areas restrict plastic.
  • Never feed or approach wild animals, including monkeys at viewpoints.
  • Ask before photographing people, especially in tribal settlements, and buy crafts directly from makers where you can.
  • Keep away from waterfall edges and fast water, especially after rain.
